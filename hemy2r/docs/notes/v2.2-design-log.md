# HEMY 2R (HEMY v2 restyle): v2.2

Based on [HEMY](https://github.com/polvinc/HEMY) by polvinc, GPL-3.0.

Current design (cleaned up 2026-11-13). A HEMY v2 harmonic mount in a 125 mm cube shell with a SAL-33-style exterior: one-piece L-plate frame, 6 mm panels, M4 corner cubes, a lid handle and a counterweight far wall. Internals (HBK17 100:1 reducers, motors, pulleys, belts, board) are stock HEMY v2; only the shell, frame, base, dovetail hardware and a few positions change.

**Status (2026-11-17):** LA-40B measured (2026-11-15) and **HBK17 reducer #1 measured (2026-11-17): matches the datasheet and the factory CAD** (Ø19 bore; input collar Ø25 × 7, plain bore, 3 × M3 set screws; Ø45 hub, Ø64 flange, Ø80 body, Ø84 plate; Ø54 pattern with 20 positions at 18°; 56.5 total). **Dovetail bay designed 2026-11-18** (section 4, `tools/v22_dovetail_bay.py`: jack + LED in aluminium, CH340E in a red-anodized insert). **Brake (2026-11-18):** brake board (U1 SDB628 out of stock → **MT3608**, U2 → **DRV103H C1556557**, P1 from My Inventory ~Nov 28), coupling (part 13) and brake supports go in the one combined JLC order; only the **SWB-003 brake** is bought later. Counterweight on every session with the FRA300 until it's fitted. **Housing chosen 2026-11-18:** this v2.2 design (JLCCNC quote $583.74, `restyle/quotes_2026-11-18/`) over polvinc's original. Next: dovetail screw re-layout, internal wiring layout. Section 10 lists the to-dos.

## 3. Design decisions
- **125 mm cube, one-piece L-plate.** RA reducer moved 6 mm toward the back wall: shorter payload arm, more room behind the RA motor, RA axis almost centred under the cube. Reducers stay half-embedded as in polvinc's design (fully-outside reducers are a v3 idea).
- **Board turned 90°** so its edge connectors face side plate A; it sits under the lid at X −17…35. Board spacers move with it.
- **12 mm M4 cubes in all 8 corners**, so every panel and lid screw is M4. Cubes are cheap and sacrificial and keep the panels plain 6 mm plates.
- **Handle kept**, moved to X −11 (in line with the USB-C window) with countersunk flat-head screws (2026-11-14). The model still shows the old button heads: they clear the board by 5.2 mm and the DEC side by 1.0 mm; flush flat heads add ~2.8 mm to both.
- **GPS back (2026-11-16, after the v2.3 window-lid study).** The board's own GPS (U3 Quectel L76-LB, kept on the JLC order) with a **25 × 25 mm ceramic patch antenna on a short IPEX (U.FL) lead** in the lid pocket at (−40, 0), lead through the Ø4 hole to **JP1**. The non-metallic "HEMY 2R" sticker (40 × 32 mm, R3, vinyl + laminate) covers the pocket, long side along the handle. OnStepX reads the GPS at boot (time + site); the iPhone still works as a fallback. (Dropped 2026-11-14, filled with `v22_gps_remove.py`; re-cut with `v22_gps_restore.py`.)
- **Base: flange + base plate on the LA-40B** (modelled 2026-11-11). Stack top to bottom: **flange 80 × 80 × 19** (Z −27.5…−8.5) → **base plate 80 × 80 × 11** (Z −38.5…−27.5), 0.5 mm chamfers meeting in a fine V split line → LA-40B top plate in the plate's recess.
  - **Flange:** reducer interface copied from A11 (12 × M3 at Ø54, counterbored from below; empty positions at 0/90/180/270° match the reducer's four larger features). Flat bottom; 4 × M5 corner threads at (±33, ±33) from the RA axis.
  - **Base plate:** 3 mm recess underneath for the LA-40B plate: **measured 2026-11-15: 50 × 54–55 × 8–9 mm, corners ≈ R3–4, 8 × M6 (corners on 38 × 38, mid-edges at ±17.5) + 1/4"-20 centre, as Angeleyes' interface drawing**; recess 56.5 × 56.5 R4.5 (square, so the plate fits either way round); 4 × countersunk M6 into the LA-40B's corner threads (±19, ±19); 4 × M5 clearance holes with Ø9 × 5.5 counterbores from below at the corners, outside the LA-40B plate.
  - **Order:** plate onto the LA-40B (M6 from above) → flange onto the reducer on the bench (12 × M3 from below) → mount onto the plate, 4 × M5 up from below.
  - **4 × M5 corners are enough:** ≈20 kN clamp vs ≈0.2 kN tipping pull; friction ≈150–200 N·m vs a few tens of N·m RA reaction. Mid-edge extras skipped (the measured 55 mm plate leaves no room; 4 corners are enough).
  - **Square 80 × 80 chosen (2026-11-15).** ~~Round Ø84 neck vs square 80 × 80: decide with the real LA-40B plate.~~ With a single jack the jack face fits a Ø84 flat; the plate screws would move to r ≈ 36 and their counterbores must clear the LA-40B plate (works if it's smaller than ~60 mm where the screws sit).
  - **Jack bay and slot** (machined from the flat bottom, closed by the base plate): keep the slot narrow; the solid around it ribs the 1.5 mm floor under the reducer pocket (don't merge or widen). Consider a shallower slot to thicken that floor. Leads go up the reducer's **Ø19 H7 through-bore**.
- **Dovetail:** polvinc's saddle and clamp kept (dual Vixen + Losmandy). His M6 clamp screw passes through the jaw, crosses under the groove and threads into the saddle body (Ø5.2 tap drill, Y 25.5 → 6; ≈14.5 mm engagement); tightening pulls the knob face against the jaw. Changes: jaws in bronze or brass, knurled D20 knobs (section 7). **Knob head must stay ≤ Ø32**: the knob axis is 17.3 mm from the body face's plane as DEC turns.
- **Knobs east via the DEC home pose, no dovetail change** (2026-11-11). The saddle is symmetric along its groove, so the knob side at home is set by the DEC position. Turned 180° in DEC from the CAD pose, the knobs face east (right for a right-hander loading from the south, as in polvinc's photo) and the dovetail's CH340/ACC face points west. At assembly, route the DEC wires untwisted in this pose and set it as home in OnStepX.
- **No TMC5160 driver heatsinks** (not needed at 0.8–0.9 A).
- **Weight:** about 4.6 kg without the base, ~5.8 kg with the LA-40B, flange and plate.
- **RA load budget:** HBK17 100:1 rated 31 N·m (max average 51, start/stop peak 70, momentary 143; listed backlash ≤ 10″, but the seller says real backlash is ~40–50″: negligible in RA, handle DEC with PHD2 one-direction DEC guiding or backlash compensation and a slight DEC imbalance). Output M3 threads are 6 mm deep: check screw engagement. Dovetail face ≈ 0.10 m from the RA axis. FRA300 rig ≈ 4.5–5.5 kg with its balance point ≈ 0.07 m above the dovetail → ≈ 7.5–9 N·m without a counterweight (≈ 25–30% of rated). Same class as the Proxisky Ragdoll 17 Pro (< 40 N·m).

## 4. Power and data
- **Single 12 V input at the base** (decided 2026-11-13; like polvinc's original): one 10 A 5.5 × 2.1 panel jack + one dim red LED on the flange (side B face). Inside the box: **10 A main fuse** → junction → **5 A fuse → mount board** and **7.5 A fuse → ACC line** (18 AWG; E5, 2026-11-14: the cooled camera will run off the StellaVita, so ACC is sized for it; confirm with the T5 current measurement; a hard ACC short may also blow the 10 A main, accepted) up the DEC bore → **dovetail 10 A ACC jack** → StellaVita. A scope-side short blows only the ACC fuse. Typical draw ≈ 5–7 A, worst case ≈ 10 A. Field kit: one 10 A cable from the Jackery (check its plug rating; cheap 5.5 × 2.1 plugs are often 5 A). No DC splitter cables.
- **Jack:** 10 A 5.5 × 2.1 panel socket, M12 thread 9 mm + body 9 mm, Ø14 bezel, 18 AWG leads, tethered cap; screwed into a tapped M12 hole (no nut). Confirm the M12 pitch on the real jack.
- **Dovetail USB-C (main control link):** CH340E module **"BTE17-06"** (16 × 16 mm, 4 holes on 9.5 × 13 mm, likely M2; **Type-C variant**) in the red insert, its own USB-C in a window in the insert's 0.8 mm skin (decided 2026-11-17/18 over an M12 waterproof bulkhead: no room for the bulkhead's inner C-to-C cable, and no CC-passthrough risk); tethered silicone dust plug; right-angle cable at the saddle. Only **UART** crosses DEC (TXD, RXD, GND), not USB data, so thin twisting wires are fine. Solder wires straight to the pads; use **TXD, RXD, GND only** (don't connect +5V or 3V3; DTR open). Needs its own hardware serial port on the board, separate from the Wi-Fi module's.
- **Logic level:** the module has no 3.3/5 V jumper and is likely 5 V TTL; the Teensy 4.0 is 3.3 V only. Bench check (section 8); if TXD idles at ~5 V, add **1 kΩ in series + 2 kΩ to GND** on module TXD → board RX.
- **Backup USB-C: window to the board's own USB-C (2026-11-14, replaces the panel-mount extension).** CN2's face is 0.05 mm inside side plate A, so no extension plug fits inside; instead a 13 × 7.5 mm window in side A exposes CN2 directly, ~5.2 mm deep. Use a cable with a slim plug body (≤ ~12.5 × 7 mm); tethered silicone dust plug when not in use. Full control port (NINA/PHD2/ASCOM/INDI) and the firmware port; never passes through DEC. OnStepX accepts several ports at once; don't run two control programs together. **CN1 and DC1 are left off the board** (2026-11-14): take CN1 (PJ-327E-SMT) off the JLC BOM, don't fit DC1 (hand-soldered); solder the dovetail UART (TX, RX, GND) to CN1's pads, leave its +5 V pad open, and tie the wires down. Bench power through IN1.
- **Dovetail bay (designed 2026-11-18, `tools/v22_dovetail_bay.py`; parameters at the top, re-run with measured parts; `tools/v22_dovetail_bay_asm.py` puts the result in the assembly):** polvinc's open side bay is filled (and his two Ø2.5 holes in the groove floor). Coordinates in the CAD pose; port face Y −50 (west at home).
  - **Port face:** **10 A jack** at X −92.5, Z 58 (tapped **M12, modelled M12 × 1, Ø11 tap drill: confirm the pitch**, in a 6 mm aluminium wall; Ø13 body bore to Y −28.5); **LED** Ø3.1 at X −83, Z 62.5 (6 mm wall, inserted from inside); **red insert** above them: face X −97.6…−80.6, Z 66.6…84.9, with the CH340E's **own USB-C** at X −92.5, Z 75.75 (vertical 9.1 × 3.4 window in a 0.8 mm skin; the USB-C nose sits in the window so plug side loads go into the aluminium). 3 mm silver cover strip at the back edge. Tethered silicone dust plug in the USB-C.
  - **Why an insert (2026-11-18):** the thin parts and every dimension that depends on the bought CH340E live in a small replaceable part, so the dovetail can be ordered before the module is measured. The jack stays in the aluminium (no room for its nut in an insert; better for 10 A heat and plug twisting). Red anodize instead of POM (POM only comes in black/white).
  - **T-slot (cut from the back, 3-axis):** face opening Z 66.5–85, inner Z 65.5–87 (2 mm lips), floor X −97.7 (4.3 mm above the groove floor), ends at Y −29.5 (the insert bears on it when a plug goes in). Wiring space below it (Z 60.5–66) for the jack lugs and the LED. 2.5 mm between the jack's thread and the slot.
  - **Cover:** 3 mm, counterbore 3 deep, open to the port face, 2 × M2.5 at (Y −27, Z 62.5 / 79.5), 5 deep tapped. **Back tab raised** from Z 84.6 to 90.5 (same 45° chamfer) to carry the taller bay; 2.1 mm rim above the cover.
  - **CH340E (BTE17-06, seller drawing):** lies flat on the insert floor, USB-C up toward the cover; 2 × M2 into the insert floor (holes 1.5 and 11.0 mm from the USB edge, 13 apart across it); pads (3V3 DTR RXD TXD +5V GND) on the far edge, wires out through the insert's rear slot. Kapton under it.
  - **Wires to DEC:** a 9.5 mm notch (Z 66–75.5) joins the bay to polvinc's channel under the reducer hub (X −86.5…−90) and on into the bore: ACC pair + 4-core.
  - **Dovetail screw re-layout:** keep the three Ø54 positions nearest 180° (Y ≈ −27, Z 61–79) empty: they'd hit the bay/notch/cover screws.
  - **Check on arrival (re-run the script with these):** see the "HEMY Measure Checklist" project doc (CH340E board thickness, shell height, nose overhang; jack pitch, length, bezel, lugs; LED).
  - Sections: `docs/dovetail_bay_sections.png`. Snapshots: `_snapshots/v2.2_2026-11-17_before_dovetail_bay.zip`, `_snapshots/v2.2_2026-11-18_before_red_insert.zip`.
- **Wireless:** LilyGO T-01C3 Wi-Fi as a second link.

## 5. Counterweight far wall
The only far wall (decided 2026-11-11).
- **Socket:** SAL-33-style lug outside, 32 × 24 mm, R4, 12 mm proud, on the DEC axis (Y 0, Z 70). **M12 × 1.75** through lug and wall (~18 mm of thread), tapped by JLCCNC. **M12 cap** with thread ≤ 12 mm for when the bar is off (longer pokes into the box).
- **Load path:** foot inside along the bottom edge (X 40–50.5, Y ±40, Z 9–19), held by **3 × M5 countersunk screws up through the floor** (X 45, Y −28 / 0 / +28) into M5 threads (8 deep), plus the 4 M4 corners.
- **Bar and weight:** Angeleyes stainless bar Ø20 × 164 mm (M12; confirm the thread) and the 5 kg weight; bar-end stop already owned. With a 5.5 kg rig the weight balances about **mid-bar**. The weight can't hit the mount anywhere on the bar; check tripod-leg clearance at home and through the meridian.
- **Balance:** no clutch (not needed on a strain-wave mount; rough balance within ~1 N·m is enough). Unpowered, the reducer and motor detent resist, so **bracket it**: slide the weight in until the scope side drifts down, out until the counterweight side drifts down, mark the midpoint on the bar. Belt off makes it more precise. Never plug or unplug a stepper with the driver powered. **Then offset RA ≈ 0.5 N·m east-heavy** (≈ 1 cm of weight travel; flips with pier side) so the gears stay loaded one way (M5: 40–50″ backlash ≈ 11 px at 4.1″/px). Load or unload the scope only with the motors powered and holding, or with the counterweight off. **Keep hands off the knobs during slews** (M10: the knob passes 7 mm from the body).
- **Without the brake:** with too little counterweight (e.g. 1–1.5 kg, ~6 N·m scope-heavy) a power loss can let the scope swing down. Balance properly, or fit the brake (check its position: on the motor side it gives ~20 N·m at RA, on the reducer input only ~6).
- **Build options (decide on cost):** one piece from a ~29 mm block, or three parts (6 mm plate + bolt-on lug + foot bar, v1.1 C10 style).

## 6. Home-position index marks (checked from side A, 2026-11-15)
90° V-grooves, 0.3 mm deep, 8 mm long, filled with white engraving crayon or paint pen.
- **Home** = counterweight down, scope parallel to the RA axis, **knobs east** = DEC turned 180° from the CAD pose (section 3). You stand south, right-handed, facing the pole; the knobs and side A face you on the east, and all the cables you plug in each night (base 10 A input, dovetail USB-C and ACC) are on side B (west).
- **Side A carries both home marks:** DEC at Z 70, 8 mm in from the back edge (X −69…−61), meeting the dovetail's single mark on its knob face (Z 70, X −88.5…−80.5); RA at X −6, 8 mm up from the bottom edge, meeting an 8 mm mark at X −6 on the base flange's side A face.
- **Side B (power side): no marks.** RA marks removed from the plate and the flange 2026-11-15 (`tools/v22_unmark_sideB.py`); DEC mark removed 2026-11-16 (`tools/v22_unmark_sideB_dec.py`), since it only met the dovetail with the knobs west, which isn't home.
- **Read it right:** RA also lines up 180° away (counterweight up). Home is marks aligned **and** knobs east **and** counterweight down.
- Renders: `docs/home_sideA.png`, `docs/home_sideB.png`.

Use a small torque screwdriver (0.5–5 N·m). Panels only need to seat: don't chase more clamp.

## 8. Build and assembly notes
- **Reducers:** RA into the floor first, then slide the DEC reducer into the back wall. Clamp screws from outside (countersunk), hex nuts inside, only at the 10 holes with a matching frame hole; the reducer's counterbored holes are its own factory screws.
- **Corner screws:** M4 × 10 everywhere, so the three screws meeting in one cube stop short of each other.
- **Brake stack, from day one:** both brake supports, the 5 × 40 shaft on two 625ZZ, the 16T pulley and the **200 mm** RA belt over all three pulleys; tension once. Until the SWB-003 (24 V) is fitted, leave the coupling (part 13) off so the 16T idles.
- **Brake board:** plugs onto the main board's BRAKE header. On first power-up, **measure ≈ 23 V at its output before connecting the brake**.
- **CH340E bench check:** USB powered, measure **TXD → GND at idle**: ~3.3 V → wire directly; ~5 V → the 1 kΩ / 2 kΩ divider. Wire TXD → board RX, RXD → board TX, GND → GND. Practise soldering on scrap wire and a spare module first (63/37 rosin-core, flux, ~330 °C, heat-shrink every joint).
- **DEC wiring:** 4-core **26–28 AWG high-flex** cable (TX, RX, GND + spare) and the ACC pair in fine-stranded **18 AWG silicone**. Leave **~80–120 mm of free twist length** through the bore; **tie down both ends right behind the joints** so flexing never happens at a solder joint. Crimped JST (PH/XH) at the board end so the dovetail unplugs. OnStepX serial at 9600–115200 baud.
- **Internal wiring layout** (with the reducer in hand): every lead tied to fixed points (M3 tie-down holes, ≤ 3.5 mm deep, on the panels' inside faces; keep off side plate A's 1.4 mm relief web) and clear of the 70T pulleys and belts; no external belt cage needed (the panels enclose them). **Bore liners:** the base leads pass the RA reducer's spinning input bore (~100× RA speed in slews) and the dovetail leads the DEC bore; if wires can touch a spinning surface, fit a thin PTFE/nylon liner fixed to the non-rotating side. Lever connectors (CMK-642, 47.6 × 15.7 × 11.2) as the power junction only if they fit; check they can't mate flipped (polarity).
- **Power switch (TechEss 16 mm latching):** find the contact pair that closes when latched, wire it to the PS1 pads (VIN and EN), insulate the rest. Hand-tighten the 19 mm nut before the lid goes on. **Checked 2026-11-17:** M16 × 1, ~10 mm thread through the 6 mm lid leaves ~1 mm spare after the nut; if the nut can't catch 3 full turns, leave the O-ring off.
- **Lid:** the board hangs from it; lift it straight up for bench work. (With CN1 and DC1 fitted they sat in side A's relief and the lid had to be tilted ~12–15° on its side A edge first; both are now left off, 2026-11-14.) CN2 sits 0.05 mm inside the box behind the side A window, so it never catches.
- **Desiccant:** small silica-gel pack inside against dew.
- **Electronics reminders:** B1 must be an **ML1220** (rechargeable; never a CR1220). Never power DC1 and IN1 together. NEMA17 holes may be only 4 mm deep: a thin washer under the M3 FM spacers. Mount the DEC motor with its leads toward −Y. Keyed headers only.
- **Firmware (OnStepX):** GPS on (Serial2, read at boot); time also kept by the Teensy clock (B1), sites saved in OnStepX, new sites from the iPhone; reducer ratio 100:1 × belt ratio; set home in the knobs-east pose. **Motor current (F1/E8):** IRUN ≈ 400–500 mA, IGOTO 800–900 mA, IHOLD ≈ 300 mA (≈ 2.5 W per motor tracking instead of ≈ 8.7 W in the sealed box; confirm no missed steps at the worst imbalance and on a cold night, T11). **Axis limits (F2/M6):** RA and DEC ≈ ±120° to limit cable twist through the bores. **Driver CLK pins (F3/E9):** net Mx_M4 (driver pin 6) driven low from boot, or 10 kΩ pull-downs. **Dovetail UART (F4):** Serial1 at 9600–38400 baud. Check the exact Config.h names in your OnStepX version.
- **Control ports, separate channels (2026-11-14):** side A USB-C = CN2, the Teensy's native USB; dovetail = CH340E UART (TX, RX, GND only) through the isolator to the CN1 pads = **Serial1**; Wi-Fi (LilyGO) = **Serial4**. OnStepX listens on all enabled channels at once. **Cautions (revisit at firmware setup):** never wire the CH340E's USB D+/D− to CN2 (two hosts on one device port; polvinc's experimental passthrough); don't run two control programs at once (e.g. NINA on a laptop and the StellaVita both commanding); confirm in polvinc's pin map that the second serial channel is enabled and mapped to Serial1, at 9600–38400 baud (F4); Serial2 (GPS UART) is spare only if U3 is left off the JLC order.
- **Commissioning:** PHD2 Guiding Assistant with guiding off, ~30 min near the celestial equator = our own PE report; let it measure DEC backlash. Before trusting GOTO, slew slowly through home and the meridian to check counterweight and cable clearances.
